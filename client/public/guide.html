Direct Strike counter guide for StarCraft II

Updated June 15, 2026 · Written for players using Starcraft Coach

Direct Strike is not ladder with a different map. Teams pick races across three waves, unlock tech over time, and fight under platform and economy rules that punish copying a 1v1 build order. This guide explains how counters work in that mode and how to use Starcraft Coach between fights without freezing when the next wave drops.

What makes Direct Strike different

In standard StarCraft II matchmaking you control one race from the start and climb a tech tree at your own pace. Direct Strike splits the match into waves. Each wave can be a different race. Your “answer” to an enemy composition depends on which of your waves is active, what tech you have unlocked, and whether ground or air platform slots are already full.

That means a unit that is a hard counter on a ladder chart can still be the wrong call mid-game: you might not have the building yet, you might be out of slots on that lane, or the fight might start before the unit finishes. Good Direct Strike play is less about memorizing one perfect counter and more about choosing the best unit you can mass now.

Waves, race picks, and wave shift

Most Direct Strike games revolve around three waves. Opponents reveal armies over time; you should track each wave separately instead of lumping everything into one list. Tagging “Marine” on wave 1 and “Battlecruiser” on wave 3 are different problems for different races on your side.

Wave shift matters when your team is ahead or behind the enemy’s wave clock. If your active army is answering an enemy wave that is not the same index you tagged, suggestions need to map to the correct team race. In Starcraft Coach, set wave shift so enemy tags line up with the team wave you are actually playing. Wrong mapping is one of the fastest ways to get “correct” counters for the wrong race.

Tech tiers (T1–T3) and locked counters

Tech unlocks are the spine of Direct Strike decision-making. Early waves often live in T1–T2. Later waves open T3 options that can swing a fight, but only if you survive long enough to unlock and produce them.

When you set your max unlocked tech in Starcraft Coach, suggestions prioritize counters at or below that tier. Higher-tech answers still appear as locked options so you can plan the next building without pretending you can build Brood Lords on T1. A practical rule:

  • If the fight is imminent, mass the best unit you can already produce.
  • If you have a quiet window, spend toward the unlock that enables a cleaner hard counter.
  • Do not park minerals waiting for a dream unit while the platform fills with something the enemy already beats.

Hard counters, soft counters, and coverage

A hard counter is a unit that generally wins the matchup when numbers are comparable. Soft counters help but may need numbers, support, or positioning. In Direct Strike you often need both: a primary path for the next production cycle and soft options that salvage an army you already built.

Coverage is the question “do I already have enough of the answer?” If the enemy massed Mutalisks and you already have Hydralisks on the board, you may only need to top up. If you have nothing that trades well, you are uncovered and should redirect production immediately. Starcraft Coach shows covered / partial / gap style coverage so you can see whether you are reinforcing or rebuilding.

Platform limits: ground vs air

Direct Strike platform capacity forces tradeoffs ladder players forget. Flooding ground slots while air is empty (or the reverse) wastes income. Before you queue twenty of a unit, ask:

  1. Is this unit ground or air?
  2. Do I still have slots on that lane?
  3. Is there a slightly worse counter that uses the lane I actually have open?

Sometimes the “best” counter on paper is unavailable because the lane is full. The next-best counter that still fits your tech and platform is the real play.

Race-agnostic habits that win more games

Tag what you see, not what you fear

Panic tags (“they might have Carriers”) create bad suggestions. Update after each reveal and after big fights. Remove units that died if your tool tracks counts; stale high counts push you into overproducing the wrong answer.

Commit early enough to finish

Switching counters every ten seconds means nothing finishes. Pick a primary path for the current tech, queue a meaningful batch, then reassess. Direct Strike rewards decisive mass more than constant theorycrafting.

Respect splash and support

Many Direct Strike armies are dense. Splash, healers, and detection change the value of a counter. If the coach suggests a unit that dies to splash in a blob, combine it with the support units your race can field at that tier—or choose a more durable soft counter you can mass safely.

Scout the next unlock

When you are about to hit T2 or T3, glance at locked counters in the coach. If the enemy’s composition is clearly answered by a unit one tier up, prioritize that unlock instead of dumping the last of your minerals into a soft answer that will be obsolete in thirty seconds.

How to use Starcraft Coach during a match

  1. Open the web tool or Windows overlays before the first fight.
  2. Set your team races and the tech tier unlocked on each wave.
  3. Tag enemy units by wave with approximate counts as scouting comes in.
  4. Read the primary counter and coverage. Produce toward the gap first.
  5. After the fight, update tags and tech. Re-check whether the next wave needs a different race answer or a new unlock.

On Windows, the desktop app’s enemy and team panels can sit over the game. Use StarCraft II’s Fullscreen Windowed mode so overlays stay visible and movable. Those panels are play utilities; this guide page is the long-form reference.

Common mistakes

  • Using ladder-only intuition and ignoring Direct Strike tech locks.
  • Tagging all enemy units on one wave when they are split across three.
  • Leaving wave shift wrong so suggestions use the wrong team race.
  • Overfilling one platform lane while the other is empty.
  • Waiting for a perfect T3 counter while a massable T1/T2 unit would stabilize the current fight.
